Stop retyping the card. Build it once.

The AI menu builder is where the catalogue lives. Categories can carry a colour and an image, so a lunch board and a bar list do not look like the same grey sheet. Each item holds a price, calories where you want them, and simple flags — veg, spicy, featured — plus availability by Breakfast, Lunch and Dinner. A breakfast sandwich can drop off the board at 11 without anyone remembering to hide it.

Import from photo reads a printed menu and builds the categories and items for you. Photograph the card, let the AI menu builder draft the structure, then correct names and prices before it goes live. A retail catalogue can come in from a Shopify CSV you already have. Catalogue profiles cover a restaurant board and a retail shop, so mixed counters are not forced into one set of fields.

A restaurant owner uses it for thalis. A café owner uses it for the pastry case. A bar manager uses it for the spirits list. A shop owner uses it for sizes and the product that should be featured on the counter. Same builder. Different catalogue.

A table QR, not a wave across the room

Contactless ordering in NHY is a per-table QR code. The guest scans it with the phone camera and lands on a web menu. There is no app download. They pick a portion size — Quarter, Half or Full — apply a coupon if you have one running, and see a live summary with subtotal, service charge, tax and discount before they submit. That screen is the difference between a surprise bill and a bill the table already agreed to.

Restaurant

The family that cannot catch a server orders the thali at Full and the child’s portion at Quarter. The ticket is already in the pipeline.

Café

A laptop table scans, adds a pastry, and stays seated. Staff stop doing laps just to take a second coffee.

Bar

A round orders from the table QR instead of waiting out a three-deep bar. Portion size covers the shared plates.

Retail shop

The same QR works at a counter display: scan, confirm the total with tax and any discount, then submit. No separate ordering app.

This QR code ordering system stays tied to the live catalogue. If an item is unavailable for Dinner, it should not still be orderable at 8pm. That is the point of building the menu in the same app that prints the code.

Pending, preparing, ready, done

Once the order lands, it moves through a short pipeline: Pending, then Preparing, then Ready, then Completed. Receipts can be sent from the same flow. Filters cover date, table, payment method and status, so a bar manager can pull tonight’s card payments and a café owner can see which table is still waiting.

The owner dashboard shows the same statuses beside today’s revenue, today’s orders and average order value. Billing, team accounts and store settings sit in the same set, so adding a closer is not a call to a vendor. Plans range from a free tier up to Enterprise.

Know the shelf before the guest does

Running out mid-shift is a floor problem and a back-room problem. Inventory covers food stock and retail stock, low-stock alerts, purchase orders, and transfers between locations. A restaurant sees the chilli oil before dinner. A café sees the oat milk. A bar sees the well spirit. A shop sees the size that sells out on Saturday and raises a purchase order instead of discovering the gap at the counter.

Alerts sit on the dashboard, not in a Monday report. This is the retail inventory management app path and the kitchen stock path in one place — what a café with a bottle shelf, or a bar with a food menu, actually needs.
